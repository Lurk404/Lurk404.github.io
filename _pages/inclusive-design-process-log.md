## Appliance Show and Tell

![A stovetop showing the knobs and controls with pieces of tape on several knobs to indicate which knob controls which burner.](/assets/images/wp/2024/10/6009-Activity1-1-scaled.jpeg)

**Problem definition:** Confusion about what burner is turned on due to lack of clear labelling. Secondarily, the temperature of oven is difficult to determine as labels have worn off.

**Induction techniques:** I considered other ovens I had used in the past and did some cursory search of modern appliances online. I spoke to the other user of the stove, my partner. They expressed even greater confusion than I experienced, admitting to use a trial and error method to determine which knob controls which burner.

![](/assets/images/wp/2024/10/6009-Activity1-2-e1729403390614.png)

**Prototyping:** I began with some odd sketches. I had previously added tape to indicate which knob controlled which burner but my partner has not found it helpful. Ultimately, I wonder if one knob being physically higher than the other would be useful. This would also serve as a multi-sensory affordance.

**Evaluation:** In discussion with peers and reading other posts I would say confusion over knob placement relative to function is common. Different manufacturers take many different approaches.

## Quad Ideation

### Transit Counter

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Expand for details of transit counter quad ideation</summary>

**Problem definition:** I often observe commuters rush down the stairs to try and catch a train to end up panting for 15 seconds before the door close or just miss the closing doors. I tend to walk at a regular pace as I am not in much of a rush. However, I would still appreciate knowing how much time, down to the second, is left before the train departs.

![Seabus departure countdown timer showing Next seabus in 11:36](/assets/images/wp/2024/10/images.jpg)

**Induction techniques:** I took mental notes about various commuters and their behaviour attempting to make it to a departing train. I considered that the same transit system (albeit different mode) employs a countdown timer. I wondered about applying the same practice used for the Seabus (pictured above) to the various train lines.

**Prototype:**

    <td><strong>Description:</strong><br/>Lack of information, signage.</td>
  </tr>

### Informed Empathy

**Problem definition:** Technical guidance fatigue and ineffective compliance-based measures result in lack of improvement in digital accessibility.

**Induction techniques:** I used my experience hosting workshops for instructors and staff as well as discussions with other facilitators. Examining attendance rates over time and attendee feedback informed the initial ideas.

**Prototyping:** I began sketching quad ideations. I spoke with my supervisor and other facilitators about what might be a useful direction to pursue.

![Initial planning sketch of quad ideation. The final version is revealed below.](/assets/images/wp/2024/10/6009-QuadIdeation-Sketch.png)
![Second sketch of quad ideation. The final version is revealed below.](/assets/images/wp/2024/10/6009-QuadIdeation-Sketch2.png)
![Final sketch of quad ideation. The final version is revealed below.](/assets/images/wp/2024/10/6009-QuadIdeation-4.png)

---

**"Sketch":**  
Instructors/creators/designers/developers unaware of minimal effort, maximum impact choices they can make to improve (digital) accessibility

**Description:**  
Technical guidance fatigue  
Workload  
"Another buzzword like DEI, UDL"  
Disconnected from "hypothetical" user with disability

---

**Audience:**   
The choices that anyone creating or distributing digital content has an immense impact on anyone consuming, interacting with, or engaging with said content. These choices have an immense impact on users with disabilities using assistive and/or adaptive technology.

**Proposed Solution:**  
Raise awareness of easy to achieve accessibility improvements through the use of anonymized personas based on real user experiences.

---

**Evaluation:** This quad ideation was iterated into a visual metaphor, shown below. As this idea potentially forms a major theme for my major research project continuous iteration is expected.

## Shared Intentionality

**Problem definition:** Using [Erin Lee's spatial-topological synchrony, temporal synchrony, and mutual knowledge creation model](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1Y_wyBWdTOBaIfx1w8k7Ni8gEI0wOaRuM/view), evaluate the share intentionality of a fully asynchronous, hybrid, and in-person class. Use the modelling to suggest improvements to each modality and plot again.

**Induction techniques:** I relied on my experience as a remote attendee and in-person facilitator of in-person and hybrid classrooms. I considered the various discussions and comments of fellow classmates as well as other facilitators. Given my experience with technology and humans, I hoped to give special consideration to the technological restraints and the capacity of facilitators.

**Prototyping:** I began with basic sketches of the concept, followed by drawing over top the blank model provided. On these pages I jotted notes about the range of each axis and some justification for that range. I then created digital copies using the blank cube, this time adding an icon of a clock and set of hands pulling a rope to suggest the mutual knowledge would improve over time. ## Conceptual Model

### Sankey

**Problem definition:** Develop a map, model, or chart to reveal patterns common to quad ideations. The intention is to create categories that could inform project ideas and groupings.

**Induction techniques:** I read and reviewed all quad ideations posted then created a table of author initials, idea, theme/topic, and audience impacted. This left me with a lot of cumbersome data to try to connect. Some basic sorting of the table helped to build a shape.

**Prototyping:** I sketched a few ideas that began as mind maps but settled on a Sankey diagram to show the flow from author and idea to topic. This allowed viewers to find their idea and follow the path to the theme or audience. Each theme and audience would be fed by various flows. A user could then follow a different flow back to a different author. My intention was to facilitate others to make their own connections.

## Cross-Sensory Prototype

**Problem definition:** Understand possible affordances of everyday objects, sketches, storyboards, and other models and how to use those to create design mock-ups and prototypes.

**Induction techniques:** Reviewing the potential project topics and considering what of those ideas lends itself to a 3D model or visual metaphor.

**Prototyping:** Initially I jotted some ideas starting with the co-design toolkit. I then create a 3D visual metaphor using office supplies (post-it notes, paper clips). In version 1, a factory produces paper clips and the toolbox only has one kind of tool. ## Group Project

**Problem definition**: Refine initial project themes and potentially divide larger groups interested in similar topics into smaller units for project work.

**Induction techniques:** Brainstorming ideas with group. One member suggested use a formula of "how might we" + verb + audience +problem + how to generate and refine ideas. Below is a screenshot of an anonymized snapshot of the table. This method was useful to codify some of the nebulous ideas revealed through discussion. Coordinating with potential project members became difficult.

![](/assets/images/wp/2024/10/6009-ProjectInduction.png)

*Screenshot of table for idea generation with the cells blurred to protect the authors' privacy.*

Using some of the examples generated from the table, we began refining the problem statement: a lack of lived experience informing teaching materials. Even with the best of intentions, instructors may not have access to inclusive, multi-modal, desiloed, and accessible teaching material. By co-designing a toolkit with individuals with lived experience (disabilities) and creating born accessible resources, a more robust and effective set of materials would be generated.

**Prototyping:** From initial notes, some basic sketches of the potential workflow for developing the toolkit were crafted.

### First Iteration

Synthesizing and refining the disparate ideas generated during brainstorming has proved difficult when done asynchonously or synchronously without a quorum of members. However, the long notes document was reorganized and condensed into:

**Background:** Value of lived experience (co-design), silos (disciplines, bureaucracy, individuals; how systems fail when individuals don’t have diverse competencies), connecting teachers to diverse, accessible resources in multiple modalities.

**Potential problem statement:** There is a lack of lived experience informing teaching material.

**Potential** **Purpose:** Create an evergreen method to build co-designed toolkits that provide (small t) teachers with diverse, inclusive, accessible content that empowers a deslioed approached to instruction. The process of creating the toolkit should be a model for future toolkit creation relative to discipline, audience, etc.

**Potential** **Objectives:**

1. Use co-design to incorporate lived experience into teaching material
2. Generate a proof of concept co-designed set of resources
3. Create a prototype repository for the toolkit

**Potential** **Scope:** For sake of proof of concept, hyper-focus on one specific discipline and audience of learners and prototype or design workflow. Limit repository (i.e., decision tree, search, CYOA, etc.) to a single toolkit option with placeholders for other end scenarios.

### First Prototype

For a first prototype of the toolkit, I decided to use a H5P branching scenario. This no code, interactive content creator is easy to use. I created one branch for a tactile grade 3 geography class. The following video is a silent demonstration of how the toolkit would work.

<video controls="" src="/assets/files/wp/2024/10/Screen-Recording-2024-10-28-at-12.06.59.mov"></video>

Feel free to try the [multi-sensory resource toolkit prototype](https://langaratest.h5p.com/content/1292413284304308087). Select **Start the Course**, choose **Grade 3** and then **Geography**. Choose **Hands-on and tactile** to arrive at the example resource. Obviously as a proof of concept this branching scenario is not populated beyond the single example.

By allowing users to go back without restarting should allow for exploration. Branches can also come back together or end scenarios can jump to another branch. This could afford an option of finding multiple resources.

For an interface, I was drawn to the idea of a mobile wallet in which cascading tiles or cards would be presented to users as a means to switch between resource formats. A similar action of a mobile wallet is present in certain physical cardholder wallets.

![Animation of wallet pushing out a cascade of cards](/assets/images/wp/2024/11/133703_0.gif)
![Screenshot of apple wallet app showing multiple cards stacked in a cascade](/assets/images/wp/2024/11/601d80d0ab669893d5c732634afd639d_png.png)
![Sketch of cascading tile ideas](/assets/images/wp/2024/11/IMG_2469.png)

**Evaluation:** While a branching scenario could be useful, it requires users to move backward or start over to find a different resource via a different path. A search interface with filters could be more effective to provide multiple formats and reduce confusion.

As a medium fidelity example, I created an [Excel sheet to mimic a possible search/filter interface](https://ocaduniversity-my.sharepoint.com/:x:/g/personal/lmcknight_ocadu_ca/EWNOz9_7SqBDoFReSyLtErAB34Z9mPtBlp9cnY2XaE8JEQ?e=RfCGm0).
