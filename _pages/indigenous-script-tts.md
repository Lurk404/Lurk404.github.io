### Examples

Text-to-speech (TTS) software is a type of assistive technology that converts written text into spoken words, enabling users to hear digital content read aloud. This technology is particularly beneficial for individuals with reading difficulties, such as dyslexia, as it allows them to access information in an auditory format, enhancing comprehension and retention. TTS can be utilized across various platforms, including computers, smartphones, and tablets, and is commonly integrated into applications for reading documents, web pages, and even images. The most common uses of TTS are to enhance accessibility, multitasking, and language learning.

TTS, built largely on English language models do an okay job of reading English aloud. Let's consider how other languages are handled.

SENĆOŦEN is a language group spoken by people in the Pacific Northwest.

ȾEȻOSEṈ means to wash your face.

In SENĆOŦEN the word for Kingfisher is ȾETĆELE

ȾETEṈ is the word for chest.

Consult [FirstVoices SENĆOŦEN](https://www.firstvoices.com/sencoten/alphabet/startsWith?char=%C8%BE&types=word) for how these words should be pronounced.

Nêhiyawêwin or Plains Cree is a language that uses Canadian Aboriginal Syllabics.

Midnight in Cree is ᐊᐱᐦᑕᑎᐱᐢᑲᐤ

ᐊᒋᒧᐏᐣ is the word for story.

[FirstVoices Cree dictionary](https://www.firstvoices.com/maskwacis-cree/alphabet/startsWith?char=%E1%90%8A&types=word) provides audio examples of how these words should be pronounced.

Kanien'kéha or Mohawk is a language spoken in Eastern North America.

á:share is a knife. The word for eagle is á:kwe'ks. áhta is a shoe.

Refer to the [FirstVoices Kanien'kéha dictionary](https://www.firstvoices.com/kanienkeha-mohawk-en/alphabet/startsWith?char=%C3%A1&types=word) for proper pronunciation.
