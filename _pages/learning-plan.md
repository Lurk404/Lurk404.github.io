## 2026 Addendum

I began the Inclusive Design program with the following goals:

- Authority
- Teaching
- Networking
- Writing
- Conspire or obstruct

Let's review.

Unfortunately, in an era of credentialism authority is derived from documented qualifications and certifications. But with accessibility and inclusion, people are experts in their own experience. I may be able to say a guideline *suggests* x or y *should* be the ideal solution, but that does not mean my conclusions are universally ideal. Colleagues of mine working in education support roles suggest including MDes in my email signature. While few, some college faculty are more (or less) receptive to support and suggestions depending on the "authority" of the source. Barring the unforeseen, I will have achieved my 2025 deliverable of an MDes in Inclusive Design.

Given the state of post-secondary, particularly colleges such as where I currently work, any ideas and plans I had are no longer relevant. Even before the stressors of job loss, few college faculty attended professional development opportunities. Now, my department is shifting our support efforts and offering fewer synchronous workshops. As such, my intention of refreshing existing offerings is no longer a priority. However, that does not rule out other avenues for teaching inclusive design and accessibility best practices to other groups, such as students. Like many initiatives, accessibility will not become an achievable standard until it is incorporated into mainstream design. Teaching web and mobile app development, publishing, digital media, and design students about accessibility may be a fruitful path to wider adoption of inclusive design principles and more accessible experiences for all.

I am bad at networking. I am incapable of self-aggrandizing and promotion. I have added many peers and instructors as 'connections' on LinkedIn, but I lack the motivation to be social. If post-secondary continues to contract, perhaps that will be the impetus I need to be more public and boastful. I'm inclined to let my IAAP credentials lapse, though I recognize they may carry more weight if I'm job hunting.

The *[Accessibility Handbook for Teaching and Learning](https://pressbooks.bccampus.ca/accessibilityhandbook/)* has undergone constant, minor revision over the past two years. The work has been supplemented by *[Digital Accessibility On-demand](https://pressbooks.bccampus.ca/a11yondemand)* and [*Academic Accommodation Fact Sheets*](https://pressbooks.bccampus.ca/accommodationguides). I have also written [The People v. PDF](https://pressbooks.bccampus.ca/accessibilityhandbook/chapter/the-people-v-pdf/) which is far longer than any individual page of these resources, but likely not in-depth enough to merit its own resource. While I initially planned a second edition of *[Accessibility Handbook for Teaching and Learning](https://pressbooks.bccampus.ca/accessibilityhandbook/)*, I am unsure if it would be best to amalgamate my disparate resources into one or continue to propagate separate resources to specific audiences.

The above are relatively quantifiable goals. The final 'goal' I had listed in 2025 was "conspire or obstruct." But there is a third way I had not considered last year. I choose to rely on no one, do whatever I think is best, and hope no one notices. There is no value in seeking the cooperation or approval of a system intent on not changing. This applies, in my case, at both the micro and macro level. Colleagues express dismay at the slightest change, revealing not only their own ineptitude but deep held certainties that they are correct. Some of this must come from fear of losing their jobs, but they operate on Ned Flanders Sr.'s tactic of having "tried nothing and we're all out of ideas". The institution pats itself on the back for initiatives no one finds useful while enrolment continues to dwindle. So I will not fall in line nor will I carry out my lone wolf crusade. I will just do what I want until someone tells me to stop.

I'm so very tired. The idea of doing anything other than what I have been doing is a prospect I don't have the mental capacity to even imagine, let alone consider.

## (2025) Goals

### Authority

During my time in the Inclusive Design program I aim to acquire research-based authority to most effectively advocate for inclusion and digital accessibility. To work toward this, I will meaningfully engage with the course material and activities presented to me. Taking special care not to disregard an idea because it is not immediately apparent how it connects to my goals, I will curate a collection of resources, knowledge, and competencies. I will rely on the expertise of my instructors and guest speakers as well as the experience and understanding my peers bring to the program.

Upon graduating in 2026, I will have an extremely unique credential and collection of competencies. I will use this personal learning plan and my existing ePortfolio to continue to track my progress and reflect on my learning.

While I do not intend to fail anything in the literal sense, there is as much unlearning as learning to be done. I am constantly assailed by processes that don't work but are "the way it's always been done." Effectively pushing back on those forces is key.

I plan to use my subject authority to create meaningful change and promote systems that are effective in promoting digital inclusion. My new competencies and understanding will help me in supporting users of assistive technology and creators eager to create more inclusive, accessible digital content. Much of what I learn will be documented in an open-license repository.

Deliverable: MDes Inclusive Design.

Due: Constant effort.

### Teaching

One of my main goals in undertaking this graduate program is to advance my ability to teach others. I want to create an effective, repeatable, and iterative process for empowering instructors and staff (and other creators) to create and choose inclusive and accessible content. I hope to do this by revealing to creators the value of accessible content and avoid relying on legislative mandates that force them. While my role is limited to instructor staff and instructors, I would also consider teaching college or university students accessible and inclusive digital content creation.

Tentatively, my major research project will explore the most effective method to raise informed empathy among content creators. My current direction is understanding how reading fiction enhances theory of mind and social cognition. The considerable research on the psychology of learning will help guide and inform my research. I hope then to collect student stories, anonymize them through personas, and incorporate the student stories into digital accessibility support resources. My new understanding based on this advanced research will allow me to (re)develop workshops, asynchronous resources, and support systems with an element of storytelling incorporated.

Some of this effort will be the fruits of my studies at OCAD. However, I will rely significantly on the experts (including instructors and guest speakers) I encounter, networking with peers, and coordinating with my existing colleagues.

While quantitative measuring of accessibility and inclusion is difficult, considering the impact to student experience and satisfaction together with workshop attendance and asynchronous resource page visits will give some sense of impact. Using attendance and engagement statistics, in addition to feedback surveys, will inform the iterative process of instructing and raising awareness about digital accessibility. Very little of this goal will result in static, tangible products which reflects the dynamic nature of technology, education, and human beings.

As with my most of my work, any new resources will be curated into open access or open-licensed online content. One potential method would be document instruction efforts including the successes and failures, processes, resources, and solutions in an open, searchable repository for future reference and asynchronous assistance.

A secondary consideration, perhaps through independent study, will explore employing mentor-style methods to teach people how to make accessible content. Numerous examples, such as the teaching hospital dynamic, could be explored to best understand the possibilities.

Deliverable: Revised workshops and asynchronous resources as well as pilot programs for new support methods.

Due: Workshop revision by 202501; Work to revolutionize support systems is ongoing.

### Networking

Engaging with instructors, guest speakers, students, and others during my time at OCAD will be invaluable. Unfortunately, I am not particularly skilled in networking. However, my intention is to develop a network of like-minded, albeit of different professional backgrounds and lived experience, professionals for future resource sharing, collaboration, and discussion. To create such a network, consistent engagement and curation of discussion are essential. I will try to be helpful and approachable to encourage others to engage with me. This effort requires others to be willing to discuss and collaborate, which can be difficult for busy students and academics. Quantifying relationships is difficult, but perhaps engaging with a platform like LinkedIn would be a useful way to take advantage of a platform many are forced to use for professional purposes to build my network. This would allow me to consider the number of connections gained over time as a metric of success. Admittedly, I have struggle to make and keep friends. Hopefully a strong shared interest in accessibility and inclusion will carry connections during times of social fatigue. This potential network will enhance overall knowledge and competencies and facilitate the dissemination of resources and information, coordinating collaboration, and expanding the network.

Deliverable: Expanded, dependable network of professionals and users.

Due: First network by 2026; Expansion ongoing.

### Writing

I intend to write a second edition of the [Accessibility Handbook for Teaching and Learning](https://pressbooks.bccampus.ca/accessibilityhandbook/) incorporating the competencies, perspectives, and information gained throughout the Inclusive Design program. A first step is to review and glean information from the first edition with an eye to the second edition's intent. From that, I will couple existing pieces with new information and draft a new outline for the book. From the outline I will create drafts, edit, revise, review, publish, and distribute.

To start, I will use my existing resources such as the first edition of the handbook and other Pressbooks I have developed. That information will be augmented by new resources. Once completed, I will use my existing (and expanded) network to raise awareness and distribute the work. I will also attend conferences to promote the collection. As with the first edition, I will have the work added to open collections (BCCampus, etc.) as well as catalogued by libraries. I plan to continue work on [Digital Accessibility On-Demand](https://pressbooks.bccampus.ca/a11yondemand/), [Academic Accommodation Fact Sheets](https://pressbooks.bccampus.ca/accommodationguides/), and [AT User Guides](https://pressbooks.bccampus.ca/atuserguides/) to refine the messaging, technical details, and frameworks to help ensure students are provided with the most accessible and inclusive content possible. Work on each of these will inform further work on the Handbook.

Web analytics will provide a quantitative measure of page visits, however the impact of such visits are difficult to measure. I consider my satisfaction with the resource to be a useful indication of success.

As with all my resources, the second edition of the Accessibility Handbook for Teaching and Learning will be free and openly licensed.

Deliverable: Accessibility Handbook for Teaching and Learning, Second Edition.

Due: 2027

### Conspire or Obstruct

I, due to various factors, do not understand how to effectively participate in the bureaucracy of large organizations. As part of my continued education and experience I hope to better understand how to complete projects that require multiple pieces of a slow, cumbersome, sometimes intentionally obstructive, bureaucracy to cooperate.

Should I find myself unable to exist within such systems, I want to learn strategies and acquire the tools to dismantle such systems. If not the entire system, I want to dismantle the procedures and practices those systems run on.

It may initially seem counter-intuitive to require the assistance of others in this effort, but the cooperation of others (fuelled by mutual frustration with systems) is paramount. A lone wolf crusade against bureaucracy will not be effective. However, I am yet unsure how to recruit.

This effort is likely to be more failure than success, but each "misstep" will reveal a new path to wearing down the machine. Should I discover any effective methods to disrupt ineffective machines I will spend considerable time sharing my methods with all.

Deliverable: Anything better.

Due: ASAP
